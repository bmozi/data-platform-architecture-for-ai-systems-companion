# Executive Decision Brief: AI-Ready Data

## The decision in plain language

The question is not “Do we have enough data?” It is “Do we maintain information
with the right meaning, coverage, rights, history, quality, and operating owner
for this AI use—and will the system stop when those conditions fail?”

## What this can unlock

- faster, more reliable automation and AI assistance;
- reusable information products instead of repeated cleanup projects;
- traceable answers, corrections, and investment decisions;
- clearer boundaries between promising pilots and unjustified deployment.

## What it protects

It reduces the chance that speed, scale, or confident output magnifies stale,
misunderstood, incomplete, or unauthorized information. It does not approve a
model, agent, action, or production release.

## Questions before investment

1. What exact use and affected decision are we enabling?
2. Who owns the meaning, and where do teams currently disagree?
3. What population, period, and conditions are missing or weak?
4. Can we reconstruct which sources and versions shaped an output?
5. Are purpose, entitlement, retention, and prohibited uses enforceable?
6. What signal stops trust, who responds, and how is a correction propagated?
7. What separate model, action, and release decisions remain?

## Evidence packet

Require a data-product contract, semantic authority, use-specific quality and
provenance evidence, an AI-data readiness decision, operating ownership, and a
reassessment trigger.

## Decision options

Approve a bounded pilot, approve with conditions, invest in a specific
readiness gap, redesign the use, defer, or reject. “The data is in the
lakehouse” and “the pipeline is green” are not readiness decisions.
