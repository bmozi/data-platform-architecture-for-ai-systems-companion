# Responsible Amplification and Possible Futures Card

**Status:** Proposed learning tool; `PLANNED/UNRUN`  
**Domain lens:** Information and infrastructure

Use this card when today's implementation question is crowding out the larger
question: *What future could this architecture help people create, and what
must remain true while capability expands?*

This is a thinking tool, not a prediction, risk certification, legal analysis,
or claim that one future is inevitable.

## The five teaching moves

1. **Recognizable problem:** A company wants local or self-managed AI for privacy, economics, or control, but the decision is framed as a model download rather than a data, compute, facilities, labor, and community system.
2. **Plain-language analogy:** Choosing a kitchen is not choosing a recipe. The food, appliances, power, ventilation, staff, suppliers, waste, and health rules determine what the kitchen can responsibly produce.
3. **Technical structure:** Data Meaning, Provenance, and Permitted-Use Contract joined to population coverage, model portfolio, workload, compute and energy envelope, location, supply dependencies, skills, correction, exit, and evidence.
4. **Consequence when wrong:** A provider bill becomes idle hardware and scarce operations work, or a convenient hosted service hides data movement, concentration risk, environmental cost, and weak exit.
5. **Transfer:** Complete one Future Information System row and compare hosted, self-managed, hybrid, smaller-model, and do-not-build options against the same requirement.

## Part 3: Examine bias as a lifecycle, not one bad column

Representative data and removing irrelevant features can help, but neither is
a complete fairness strategy. For this exact use, ask:

- Who appears in the data, labels, examples, tests, and design room?
- Who is missing, rare, combined into an average, or measured poorly?
- Which objective, proxy, threshold, or ranking makes one outcome preferable?
- Which people receive automation, delay, scrutiny, escalation, or denial?
- Can feedback from earlier decisions make later decisions look justified?
- Can an affected person understand, contest, correct, and recover from the
  outcome?
- Which result would narrow, pause, or reverse the system?

Record observed outcomes separately from assumptions. A plausible concern is a
test proposal, not proof that harm occurred. A green aggregate is not proof
that relevant groups or conditions experienced acceptable outcomes.

## Part 4: Write three plausible futures

Imagine enterprises running specialized models near private data while renting frontier capability selectively. Trace the choice from meaning and provenance through networks, storage, memory, accelerators, cooling, power, materials, capital, jobs, and local communities.

Do not choose the most exciting story yet. Ask what evidence would strengthen
or weaken each one. Model portfolios are governed like capital portfolios; compute and energy provenance travel with data products; data, FinOps, facilities, procurement, sustainability, and policy decisions converge.

## Discussion prompts

1. Which part of this future would genuinely expand human agency?
2. Which present obstacle is technical, and which is economic, organizational,
   political, legal, or cultural?
3. If capability becomes abundant, what becomes scarce?
4. Which new job or responsibility appears because this system exists?
5. What should remain a human or institutional decision even if a model can
   make a plausible recommendation?
6. What would a hopeful critic notice that an enthusiast might miss?
7. What would a thoughtful optimist notice that a doom-centered review might
   overlook?
8. What can we design this quarter that keeps several good futures open?

## Evidence boundary

A completed card records a reasoned scenario and current decision. It does not
prove the forecast, fairness, effectiveness, safety, legal sufficiency,
environmental benefit, economic value, or reader learning. Retain the sources,
assumptions, dissent, tests, results, and review date that support any later
claim.
