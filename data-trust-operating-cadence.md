# Data Trust Operating Cadence {.field-tool-long-title}

**Use boundary:** Illustrative field tool; operating validation unrun.
**Use with:** the [Data Meaning, Provenance, and Permitted-Use Contract](data-meaning-provenance-and-permitted-use-contract.md), not instead of it.
**Does not create:** organizational authority, a compliance program, a release
approval, or action permission.

Data products fail quietly when a quality alert, a contested definition, a
policy change, or an AI-answer failure belongs to nobody. This illustrative
30-minute weekly cadence gives one named information use a place to surface its
unfinished consequence.

## Role card

One person may hold more than one role in a small team. Do not use a role name
as a substitute for a named accountable owner.

| Role | Owns | Must not decide alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decision owner | Whether the named information is still fit to inform the decision | Semantic changes, policy exceptions, or system action authority outside the assigned decision |
| Semantic authority | Meaning, metric definitions, translation, and effective-time changes | Consumer acceptance of a change they do not own |
| Data-product/platform owner | Availability, lineage, change execution, recovery, and operating evidence | Whether the information is permitted for every new use |
| Permitted-use owner | Purpose, entitlement, classification, retention, and escalation of rights issues | Technical fitness or action approval by implication |
| AI-interface owner | Question scope, answer behavior, evidence/citation configuration, and abstention handling | Business meaning, data rights, or a downstream action |
| Separate action authority | Any consequential action proposed from the information or AI answer | Data fitness or answer quality as a substitute for action authorization |

## Thirty-minute agenda

| Minutes | Question | Input | Accountable output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | What changed since the last review? | Source, schema, policy, entitlement, index, incident, and use-change notices | Changes triaged to a named owner |
| 5–12 | Is a named decision product outside its trust boundary? | Quality, freshness, coverage, and provenance exceptions | Continue, condition, stop, or revalidate decision |
| 12–18 | Did meaning, purpose, or authority change? | Semantic disputes, new consumer requests, policy changes | Accepted definition, blocked use, or escalation |
| 18–24 | Did an AI interface overreach, abstain incorrectly, or lack evidence? | Acceptance-pack tests, incidents, review samples | Fix, narrow scope, suspend, or record unknown |
| 24–30 | Who owns each unfinished consequence, by when? | Open ledger from this meeting | Named owner, due date, evidence needed, and next escalation |

## Stop-trust signals

Treat these as review triggers, not automatic proof of a defect:

- a source correction, unexplained cohort change, material freshness breach, or
  missing reconstruction link;
- a contested definition or semantic change without an effective-time decision;
- an entitlement, purpose, retention, or classification change;
- an AI answer without required evidence, a failed abstention, or a newly
  requested action; or
- a cost or service-level guardrail breach from the Decision Cost Envelope.

The owner may decide to continue conditionally only when the conditions,
affected consumers, evidence gap, and review date are visible. “The dashboard
is green” is not a resolution.

## What AI may generate

AI may draft agendas, summarize retained incidents, identify open fields, and
propose questions. It must not close a trust item, accept a semantic change,
approve a restricted use, or determine that an action is safe to take.

## Evidence boundary

This is an illustrative meeting design. It does not prove that a weekly cadence
improves quality, reduces incidents, yields compliance, or creates accountable
governance. Any future evaluation should retain the agenda, inputs, decisions,
unresolved items, negative cases, participant roles, and changes to the
template rather than reporting attendance as effectiveness.
