# Team Workshop: Is This Data Ready for This AI Use?

Use this 90-minute session to turn one vague “AI-ready data” request into a
bounded decision and evidence backlog.

## Participants

Bring the use owner, a domain meaning owner, data engineering, an AI or
application practitioner, privacy/security/governance, and one person affected
by the output.

## Agenda

### 0–15 minutes — Name the use and consequence

Complete the six-line first assessment in the
[AI-Data Readiness Assessment](ai-data-readiness-assessment.md). Do not begin
with tables, models, or platform products.

### 15–30 minutes — Resolve meaning

Write the grain, time boundary, inclusions, exclusions, and authority for the
most important term. Record disagreements; do not average them away.

### 30–45 minutes — Find missing people and conditions

Identify represented and omitted populations, periods, cases, and corrections.
Ask who bears the consequence when coverage is weak or stale.

### 45–60 minutes — Trace one answer

Follow a hypothetical answer back through source, version, transformation,
chunk or feature, index, and correction path. Mark every link that cannot be
reconstructed.

### 60–70 minutes — Check purpose and authority

Separate the right to access data from the right to use it for this AI purpose,
and separate data fitness from model, workflow, agent, action, and release
approval.

### 70–80 minutes — Define stop-trust behavior

Name the signals that cause abstention, quarantine, fallback, correction, or
reassessment. Assign an owner and response time.

### 80–90 minutes — Decide the next evidence move

Choose approve, condition, defer, or reject for the data-use boundary. Complete
one row of the [Value and Evidence Ledger](VALUE-AND-EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md).

## Read-back test

Ask the affected-person representative to explain the allowed use, known
limits, stop condition, and correction path. Record confusion as a design
finding.
